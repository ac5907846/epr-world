Presence is not performance

Having an EPR law is not associated with higher recycling on its own. The association appears only where institutions can enforce it, and only once waste collection is nearly universal.

Development reshapes the waste

As countries grow richer, organics shrink and the packaging-relevant share of waste (paper, plastic, glass, metal) doubles. The materials EPR governs are exactly the materials development creates.

Where the waste of 2050 will arise

Tiles are sized by projected 2050 municipal waste and colored by current EPR status. A fifth of future waste will arise where no binding EPR exists today, and nearly half of that sits in just five countries.

The WEEE gap

Electronic waste grows with income. Open circles are countries without WEEE-specific EPR; bubble area is national tonnage.

EPR readiness check

Pick a country to see whether the two enabling conditions this study identifies (institutional quality and near-universal collection) are in place, and what the model implies for the recycling margin of EPR there.

Estimates are conditional associations from a cross-section of 119 countries, not causal effects. Read them as a diagnostic, not a forecast.
